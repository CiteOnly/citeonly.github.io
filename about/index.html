About

CiteOnly, and the company behind it.

CiteOnly cannot make things up. Each part of an answer is a passage taken word for word from your own documents, with its source beside it. It is built by Biclay, a small company that builds AI for work where being wrong has consequences.

Where we are Early. In development and running pilots.

Who builds CiteOnly

CiteOnly is built by Biclay, which Sushant Daga founded. Before CiteOnly the work was consulting on applied machine learning, described at biclay.com: getting language-model products into production, cutting the cost and latency of systems already running, standing up self-hosted models where data could not leave the building, and helping teams build the tooling that gets an AI feature from specification to launch.

That work is where the problem came from. The same conversation kept recurring in different rooms. A team would have something working, and then somebody whose name went on the output would ask where a particular sentence came from, and there was no good answer. The usual response was to add a checker, a citation matcher or a set of guardrails on top. It made the system more complicated without making the answer any more defensible.

A checker on top means the system writes its answer first and attaches the citations afterwards. CiteOnly generates the citations themselves. Each part of an answer is a passage from your documents produced with its source, so there is nothing to check afterwards and the record of where each part came from arrives with the answer.

On the name

The name says what the system generates. It generates citations, and each one is a passage from your documents with its source. Free generation is where fabrication happens, because a model writing freely can produce a sentence that is in none of your documents. CiteOnly has no free-writing step. It cannot make things up, and every part of an answer is a citation.
