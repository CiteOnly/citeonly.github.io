An early demonstration, on financial reports

Financial answers, worked out. Nothing invented.

CiteOnly answers questions on annual reports the way an analyst does: it finds the figures, works the arithmetic through step by step, and says how sure it is of each step. This page shows it on FinQA, the public test of questions written by finance experts over S&P 500 annual reports. In September 2026 we ran an early version of CiteOnly on its 1,147 test questions, once, to show the way it answers.

Watch it work a question

What it does

It works the question out, with guardrails built in

It reasons, step by step

It chooses the figures and the operations, carries each result into the next step and arrives at the number. Finding the figure is where it starts.

Bounded to the filing

It works only with figures from the report and the question. A figure that is not there cannot appear in an answer.

The operations built in

It adds, subtracts, multiplies, divides, compares, totals and averages. It cannot invent an operation, and the arithmetic is exact.

Calibrated at every step

Every step says how sure it is, and that is how often it is right. You know which answers to check first.

About this test

The benchmark. FinQA (Chen et al., EMNLP 2021): questions written by finance experts over pages of S&P 500 annual reports, each with the expert's working. CiteOnly was tested once on its 1,147 test questions, scored by the benchmark's own evaluation code, unchanged.

Price. CiteOnly's price is quoted for your volume and documents. Reviewer cost on this page is reviewer hours at the rate on the slider, $50 an hour unless you change it.

Time to check. Built from the test set's measured answers: how many steps and numbers each answer has, and how confidence orders them. The seconds and minutes are assumptions, set by the sliders. The row for written working assumes the same number of steps and numbers as CiteOnly's, and gives it no credit or blame for accuracy, which is unmeasured here.

The answer key. Two mechanical checks over the key's own fields, run 29 September 2026. The first compares the key's written answer with the result of its own working, to the precision written, allowing percent as a fraction and ignoring sign. The second compares CiteOnly's marked-wrong numbers with the written answer, sign included. Answers such as "yes", or too coarse to tell two sums apart ("8%"), are not checked. Separately, 30 sure answers marked wrong on FinQA's development questions were read by hand against the page; that reading is ours, and no rate is drawn from it.

The demo. Six questions from the annual reports of Ball Corporation (2010), Union Pacific (2014), Loews (2015), Marathon Oil (2015 and 2007) and Lockheed Martin (2006). Page text, tables and the key's working are FinQA's; the working, the confidences and the runner-ups are CiteOnly's stored output for each question.
