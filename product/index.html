The product

Answers made of your own documents.

CiteOnly takes a set of documents you supply and answers questions about them. The answers are built from material that is already in those documents, and each part arrives with the place it came from.

What it does

You give CiteOnly a body of documents: a contract and its amendments, a policy manual, a deal room, a set of guidelines, a case file. You ask a question in ordinary language. What comes back is the material in those documents that answers it, together with the document, the location and the surrounding passage for each piece.

Three things follow from that, and each is something a general assistant cannot promise.

Scope of a pilot Agreed with you, per engagement.

I

Every sentence you read is a sentence from your documents

The answer is assembled from what your documents contain, so what you read and what the file says are the same words. Verifying an answer means opening the passage named beside it.

II

The audit trail is always present, by design

Most systems assemble their audit trail afterwards, by logging what was retrieved and matching it back to what was written. That reconstruction is approximate, and it is where trails develop gaps.

Here the trail is produced with the answer. An answer is built out of located material, so the record of where every part came from exists before anyone asks for it, complete by design: nothing to configure, nothing to maintain, and no gap between what the system did and what the log says it did.

III

When the answer is not there, it says so

If the documents do not contain an answer, CiteOnly reports that. A general assistant asked about a clause that is not in a contract will usually produce a reasonable-sounding clause, because producing something is what it does. A clear negative tells you the contract does not have the clause.

Where it fits

What it is built for, and where it stops.

  • Answers, not drafts

    CiteOnly answers questions about documents you already have. Drafting a memo, summarizing into fresh prose or rewriting in a house style means producing words that are not in the source, which is exactly what it is built not to do. For those jobs you need a different tool, and probably a person.
  • Faithful always

    CiteOnly can fall short on capability, never on faithfulness. It may hand you a passage that is in your documents but is not the one you wanted. Because every part of an answer arrives with its source, finding that out takes one look at the passage named beside it. There is no claim to investigate and no figure to re-check. A wrong answer is cheaper to catch here than in a system that writes freely.
  • Beyond search

    Keyword search returns documents and leaves the reading to you. CiteOnly answers the question and shows you the passage that answers it. The distinction matters most when the answer is spread across documents that amend one another.

Questions we get asked

Where does our data go?

CiteOnly can be self-hosted. Ask us directly and we will answer specifically.

How is this different from retrieval with citations?

Retrieval hands passages to a model that writes freely; the citation records what was nearby, not what supports the sentence. CiteOnly has no free-writing stage. The comparison is set out on the home page, and why a checking layer does not close the gap is a longer argument.

Can it work across many documents at once?

Yes, and that is where it is most useful. The awkward questions are the ones where a master document says one thing, an amendment changes it and an annex qualifies it. Each part of an answer carries its own source, so when the master agreement and the amendment disagree you see both passages side by side.

What does a pilot involve?

A set of your documents, the questions your team actually gets asked, and a way to tell us when an answer was not the one you wanted. That last part is the valuable one.
