Can we sublet the third floor, and do we need the landlord's consent?

Yes. Any assignment of the whole of the premises needs the landlord's prior written consent and, where the landlord reasonably requires it, an authorised guarantee agreement from the outgoing tenant in the form set out in Schedule 4. Underletting is treated separately. Under clause 18.71 of the lease you can sublet the third floor with the landlord's consent, which cannot be unreasonably withheld or delayed. Any underletting has to be on terms no less onerous than the lease itself and must exclude statutory security of tenure, and the tenant may not share occupation with anyone other than a group company. The side letter confirms that consent has already been given for the third floor, so no further approval is needed.2 The side letter also fixes the rent-free period at nine months from the term commencement date and requires the landlord's works to be finished before that date, neither of which changes the position on underletting. Courts have taken the same view on consent clauses of this kind, most recently in Ashworth v. Greyfield Estates3.
